Hi, I'm Badr.

I'm a curious person with way too many interests, and I kind of like it that way.

Every week, I seem to find a new rabbit hole to fall into. It might be politics, philosophy, science, a new technology, or some completely random topic I didn't know I cared about a few days earlier. I enjoy learning things simply because they interest me.

I'm also a creative person. I love making things when I get the chance, whether that's writing a Substack article, making an educational video about machine learning or computational politics on The Polynode, playing around with a design in Photoshop, or turning a strange idea into a little project. Sometimes I spend more time exploring an idea than I planned, but that's half the fun.

From Morocco to the Netherlands

I'm from Morocco, where I studied International Relations with a minor in Data Analytics for Politics at Mohammed VI Polytechnic University. I later studied at Sciences Po Lyon in France, before moving to the Netherlands for my master's in Methodology, Statistics and Data Science at Utrecht University.

Moving between Morocco, France and the Netherlands has made me appreciate how much there is to discover. I love travelling, visiting new places, and meeting people with different perspectives. And I'm always up for a long conversation about politics, philosophy, science, or pretty much anything that makes us think.

What I'm working on

Academically, I find myself somewhere between data science and the social sciences. I'm especially interested in how statistics, AI and machine learning can help us understand society and politics, and how we can make those methods more reliable.

Right now, I'm doing my master's thesis with the Methodology Department at Statistics Netherlands (CBS), studying how advanced machine learning can improve the automatic editing of business survey data.

I'm a research student assistant at Utrecht University, working on AI speech recognition for mixed-language audio.

I've also just finished a research traineeship with Utrecht University's Missing Data Group (February to November 2026). I developed a custom R imputation method that integrates glmmTMB with mice for multilevel missing-data analysis.

Outside university, I enjoy travelling, hiking, catching up with friends, creating things and discovering new interests.
